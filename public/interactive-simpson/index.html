Simpson's paradox

Sixty patients. The ones who got more nursing hours took longer to recover, so the administrator proposes cutting the nursing budget. Then you split the same sixty patients by how sick they were on admission, and every single group says the opposite. Same data, opposite sign.

What you are looking at

Each patient arrives with a severity U from 3 (mild) to 8 (severe), ten patients per level. Sicker patients get more nursing, and sicker patients also take longer to heal. Nursing itself genuinely helps: the true coefficient is -0.5, half a day of recovery bought per extra nursing hour.

severity, the confounder, never entered into the regression
U  ∈  {3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8},   ten patients each

nursing hours per day: sicker patients get more
X  =  2  +  0.8 · U  +  noise

recovery days: nursing helps, severity hurts
Y  =  5  - 0.5 · X  +  βU · U  +  noise

Both arrows out of U point the same way, and that is the whole trap. Severity pushes nursing up, and it pushes recovery time up. A regression that never sees severity has no way to tell the two influences apart, so it hands the credit to the only variable it can see. Nursing takes the blame for the illness.

Why the sign flips

Leave U out and the slope you estimate is not βX. It is βX plus a bias term: the confounder's effect on the outcome, times how tightly the regressor tracks the confounder.

omitted variable bias, recomputed live from the sixty points

The tracking factor δ is Cov(X, U) / Var(X), the slope you would get by regressing the hidden severity on the nursing hours you can actually see. In this sample it is about 0.92: nursing hours are very nearly a proxy for severity. So the bias term inherits almost the full weight of βU, and once βU climbs past roughly 0.53 it overpowers the true -0.5 and the reported sign turns positive. Nothing about nursing changed. Only the size of the thing we forgot to measure.

Drag βU to zero and the bias vanishes, even though nursing still tracks severity exactly as tightly as before. A confounder needs both of its arrows. Cut either one and the pooled estimate is honest again.

The fix

Put severity back into the regression and ask for the coefficient on nursing while holding severity fixed. On these sixty patients that gives:

multiple regression of recovery on nursing and severity together

That is the truth, recovered, and like the six group slopes it does not move when you drag the slider. The six dashed lines in the chart are the same idea done by hand: inside a severity stratum severity is constant, so it cannot confound anything, and the only thing left for the line to measure is nursing.

It happened for real

Berkeley, 1973. Across the university as a whole, men were admitted to graduate school at a visibly higher rate than women, and the case looked open and shut. Department by department, women were admitted at an equal or higher rate almost everywhere. Women had simply applied in greater numbers to the departments that admitted fewest people. Department was the severity variable. Pool over it and the sign flips.

What to take away

A coefficient is only as trustworthy as the variables you left out. The arithmetic in the administrator's chart is exact, the line is the genuine least squares fit, every number on this page is real, and the conclusion would still have cut nursing from the patients who needed it most. Before you read a sign off a model, ask what the model was allowed to see, and what you quietly pooled over.
