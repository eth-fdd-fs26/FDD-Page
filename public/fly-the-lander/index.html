What to notice

The reward is mostly not the landing

Almost every step pays a small amount for getting nearer the pad, slower and more upright, and charges you for fuel. The big +100 or −100 arrives once, at the end. Most of your return is earned before you touch down.

Doing nothing is an action

Action 0 costs nothing and is often right. Every frame of main engine costs 0.30, so a pilot who holds the button down pays for it even while flying well.

You only choose the action

You pick one of four. What happens next is the environment's business: gravity, momentum, and where you happened to start. That split is what makes this a Markov decision process rather than a puzzle.

The reward function and the eight state numbers are transcribed from the environment used in the exercise (gymnasium's LunarLander-v3). The physics here is a lightweight stand-in for Box2D, tuned so it flies like the original: a plain automatic pilot lands 19 of 24 starts with a median return of 191. Use it to build intuition, not to compare scores with the notebook.
