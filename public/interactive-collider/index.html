In the wild

Colliders in real journalism and real science

Every one of these headlines is a collider in disguise. The mechanism is the same each time: the writer (or the scientist) has unknowingly conditioned on a common effect.

Medical News Today Really hot guys are also really not nice, says science

The data: dating-app users. But the app pre-filters on being noticed at all, and attractiveness and personality are both inputs to that filter, so a negative correlation appears in the filtered sample even when none exists in the population.

Read original → Attitude New study finds that hot guys are more selfish

A press write-up of the Brunel University study framing attractiveness as a trade-off against niceness. Same collider: the dating-pool sample conditions on being noticed, so looks and kindness look negatively correlated in the filtered data even when they are independent in the population.

Read original → Internet Archive · Jordan Ellenberg How Not to Be Wrong: the Great Square of Men

Ellenberg's essay that popularised the Great Square of Men: why the nice-and-hot subset of famous people looks like a tradeoff even when, in the full population, niceness and hotness are statistically independent.

Read the book → Nature Communications · Griffith et al. 2020 Collider bias undermines our understanding of COVID-19 disease risk and severity

Early pandemic hospital data seemed to show smoking was protective against severe COVID. But being in the hospital sample is itself a collider: smoking and COVID both affect hospitalisation, so conditioning on it manufactured the paradox.
